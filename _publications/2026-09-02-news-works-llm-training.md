## Abstract

The copyright assessment of using news works in large language model (LLM) training has long oscillated between blanket infringement and blanket exemption. The underlying difficulty is that existing discussions take "whether infringement is established" as their starting point, bypassing the more prior question of how the conduct itself should be characterized. The center of gravity of copyright assessment should therefore return to the input side, with conduct characterization governing the infringement determination; the normative axis of that characterization is a relocation of the idea–expression dichotomy from the object side to the conduct side — what is regulated is not copying as such, but the object that the copying captures. News works combine the properties of public information, data elements, and intellectual creation; this third property reveals that news works have never been a uniform whole, which reduces the global question of "whether protection is warranted" to a layered identification of components within the work and stages of use. On this basis, a standard for "expressive use" can be constructed, weighing three factors — the degree of expressive exploitation, the risk of model memorization, and market-substitution harm — and applied within a four-quadrant matrix formed by crossing a behavioral dimension (technical copying / deep learning) with an object dimension (factual news / original news): technical copying of factual news is presumed non-expressive use, while deep learning on original news is presumed expressive use.

**Keywords:** LLM training; news works; idea–expression dichotomy; expressive use
