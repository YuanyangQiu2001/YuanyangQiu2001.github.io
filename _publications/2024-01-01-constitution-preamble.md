## Abstract

Any argument that a constitutional preamble has legal effect must confront the objection that preambles are difficult to apply. Viewed comparatively, constitutional systems around the world take seriously the preamble's influence on constitutional practice, and have developed a range of positions on its application — resistant, neutral, and affirmative. That comparative experience confirms the preamble's practical potential. Given the distinctive features of China's constitutional implementation system and of the preamble's own text, several institutional pathways can be pursued together: legislative application, judicial application, and application in constitutional review. In legislation, lawmakers should recognize that applying the preamble is a constitutional obligation, and should attend both to the order of values the preamble expresses and to the social and institutional legislative facts. In adjudication, judges should choose the right occasion and moment to invoke the preamble as a ground of decision and reason from it substantively. In constitutional review, reviewing bodies can draw on the two comparative models of "independent invocation" and "supplementary reasoning," applying a method of balancing and treating the preamble as authoritative evidence of constitutional principle or constitutional spirit.

**Keywords:** constitutional preamble; application of the Constitution; comparative constitutional law; constitutional review; legislative application
