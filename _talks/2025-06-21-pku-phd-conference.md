## Abstract

The informatization of privacy and the privatization of information pose new judicial challenges for establishing intrusion upon seclusion. American rules on informational privacy intrusion have been shaped by the control theory of privacy and by the typology of privacy torts. Confronting the "algorithmic turn" in privacy law, federal courts in California have recently developed a three-element test for informational privacy intrusion: actual harm, a reasonable expectation of privacy, and highly offensive conduct. This paper argues that Chinese courts should consider importing the "highly offensive conduct" element into their own analysis of informational privacy intrusion.
